About

I am a Pre-Doctoral Fellow at the Harvard Kennedy School, where I work with Gordon Hanson on the Reimagining the Economy project, where I work on various projects related to immigration, urban policy, and local labor markets, with an overarching thesis of studying local prosperity and resilience to economic shocks, and to evaluate industrial and place-based policy. I also collaborate with Brittany Street, Emily Weisburst, and Felipe Goncalves on a project examining how historical transportation infrastructure (railroads and highways) affects modern crime outcomes.

My research interests are in urban and applied microeconomics, including housing affordability and supply, neighborhood effects and segregation, transportation and spatial mobility, and place-based policy. I hold B.S. degrees in Economics, Mathematics, and Statistics from the University of Missouri, and I plan to pursue a Ph.D. in economics.

Working Papers

The Effects of Medicaid Expansion on Uninsured Rates [PDF] [Code]  ·  JEL: I13, I18, H75

Abstract. Access to healthcare is an integral factor in household financial decision-making. Medicaid expansion, legislation that each state may choose to opt into, attempts to alleviate this household burden by expanding guaranteed medical coverage to all citizens in a state below 138% of the Federal Poverty Level (FPL). I exploit within- and across-state variation through a staggered difference-in-differences framework comparing outcomes in expansion versus non-expansion states. Results indicate that Medicaid expansion causes a 34.9% decrease in the uninsured rate and a 2.5% decrease in private health insurance enrollment. While total emergency department visits remain unchanged, visits by Medicaid payers increase by 43.5% while self-pay/no-charge visits decrease by 56.5%, suggesting a shift in financial burden from households to Medicaid programs. These findings have implications for the design of healthcare safety net programs and state-level policy decisions regarding Medicaid expansion.
